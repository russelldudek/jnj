120-Day Entry Plan

Earn the right to scale by understanding the real Order Trace.

Start with workflow truth and decision boundaries, not a technology roadmap.

Entry principles

Tactical empathy

Understand the pressure, history, current strengths, and fatigue before proposing a new mechanism.

Customer-backward trace

Follow commitments and exceptions across the actual journey rather than organizing discovery by system inventory.

Evidence before scale

Use bounded interventions to prove customer, control, economic, and adoption value.

Explicit authority

Name workflow owners, technical owners, human decisions, escalation boundaries, and stop rights.

Days 1-30 - OTC Reality Map

ObjectivesUnderstand priority journeys, recurring exceptions, decision rights, controls, data boundaries, customer impact, and active transformation work.
Field workListen to executives; observe customer service and operational work; trace representative orders; review exception and dispute evidence; meet process, platform, data, quality, finance, commercial, supply, and local-market owners.
OutputsOTC Reality Map; stakeholder topology; decision-rights draft; source-of-friction hypotheses; measurement baseline; immediate-risk list.
GateNo intervention enters the portfolio without an accountable workflow owner and a testable problem statement.

Classify before building

Days 31-60 - OTC Exception Portfolio

DimensionQuestionEvidence
CustomerWhat commitment or resolution experience is affected?Disruption, complaint, delay, confidence.
RecurrenceIs this a repeated pattern or a one-time event?Frequency, aging, rework trail.
ClassData, process, policy, integration, handoff, legitimate variation, or authority?Root-cause evidence.
ValueWhat customer, flow, control, or economic outcome improves?Baseline and target range.
FeasibilityAre rules, data, integration, and ownership sufficient?Readiness and dependency map.
AdoptionWhat must people do differently?Behavior and capability burden.

Portfolio decisions

Act now

Material recurrence, clear ownership, bounded risk, usable evidence.

Learn first

Important friction with uncertain root cause, rules, data, or authority.

Stop or defer

Weak value, no owner, unstable workflow, or burden merely shifts elsewhere.

Days 61-90 - Proof and Adoption Review

Run a small number of bounded interventions inside real work. Use a weekly review that combines process evidence, technical performance, human authority, customer impact, and adoption behavior.

ScaleOutcome and behavior improve; controls hold; ownership is stable; transfer conditions are known.
RedesignThe problem is real, but the intervention adds burden, moves the exception, or misses the authority boundary.
PartnerThe value is clear, but platform, architecture, vendor, market, or compliance expertise should own part of the solution.
StopEvidence does not justify further burden or investment.

Create the scaling contract

Days 91-120 - Digital Factory Scaling Contract

Process-product ownership

Name the business owner, product owner, technical owner, control owner, adoption owner, and decision cadence.

Reuse standard

Define which rules, data contracts, integrations, controls, and operating conditions must transfer.

Value cadence

Review customer, flow, economics, trust, adoption, and learning together.

Portfolio governance

Make scale, redesign, partner, hold, and stop decisions visible and evidence-based.

120-day outcomes

  • A shared view of the priority OTC journeys and recurring exception burden.
  • A transparent portfolio with accountable owners and clear intervention classes.
  • A small number of real-work proofs with adoption and value evidence.
  • A reusable scaling standard that protects continuity, control, and local judgment.
  • An executive cadence that connects investment to customer, operational, and economic outcomes.

Risks to manage

Over-standardizationPreserve legitimate market, product, and relationship variation through explicit decision rights.
Transformation fatigueReduce burden early; reuse existing strengths; communicate stop decisions as clearly as scale decisions.
Technology-first scopeRequire workflow owner, baseline, human authority, and outcome before solution selection.
False reuseTransfer only when operating conditions and ownership transfer with the technology.

Day-120 test: Leaders can explain where the Digital Factory is intervening, why, who owns the workflow, what decision improves, what evidence proves value, and what will not be scaled.

Hypothesis-based plan for discovery; final priorities depend on J&J's internal OTC landscape and role mandate.
