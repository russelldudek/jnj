The Order Trace

Interview thesis brief | Director, MedTech Digital Factory - OTC

A Digital Factory should convert recurring friction into governed process products - making every order explainable, every exception visible, and every improvement reusable.

Mandate hypothesis

Turn fragmented, exception-heavy MedTech OTC work into a governed portfolio of digital process products that improves customer service, operating visibility, control, adoption, and economic performance without destabilizing continuity.

Operating assumption: OTC is treated as Order-to-Cash and should be validated during discovery against J&J's internal mandate and process boundaries.

Company moment

Accelerated growth

Q1 2026 J&J sales were $24.062B, up 9.9% reported. MedTech sales were $8.636B, up 7.7% reported and 4.6% operational.

Portfolio movement

MedTech growth across electrophysiology, Abiomed, Shockwave, and trauma; an Enterprise Business Review planned; and intended separation of Orthopaedics.

Central tensions

Global standard vs legitimate complexity

Standardize customer and control-critical decisions without erasing necessary market, product, and relationship judgment.

Transformation pace vs continuity

Change the system while protecting current commitments, controls, and operating stability.

Automation vs accountability

Move stable rules faster while preserving visible human authority for consequential exceptions.

Platform output vs value realization

Judge digital products by retained use, lower recurrence, customer impact, and economics - not release volume.

Strongest objection

Concern: Russell has not already led a global MedTech OTC or major SAP transformation.

Response: Do not disguise the gap. Position the transfer: he has led inside the customer, operational, technical, quality, logistics, ERP, and adoption systems enterprise technology must improve. He would partner with J&J's process, platform, architecture, data, and compliance experts while bringing operator discipline to workflow truth, ownership, standard work, escalation, adoption, and measurable outcomes.

Change burden

The model requires leaders and workflow owners to baseline work, expose exceptions, clarify decision rights, practice new behaviors, own outcomes, and return evidence. The Digital Factory must make those requirements lighter and more useful than the burden it removes.

Interview posture

Use tactical empathy before prescription. Treat the model as a hypothesis to test. Ask where the current system already works, where local complexity is legitimate, and what prior transformation attempts taught the organization.

Executive questions

  1. Which OTC exceptions create the greatest customer or patient-care consequence, and which create the greatest internal burden?
  2. Where does J&J need global consistency, and where must local market or product judgment remain different?
  3. How are Digital Factory products owned after launch?
  4. What evidence causes J&J to scale, redesign, partner, or stop an intervention?
  5. Where is transformation fatigue strongest, and what has earned trust?
  6. How does the intended Orthopaedics separation change dependencies or continuity burden?
