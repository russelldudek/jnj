Friction Compression System

Do not automate the exception maze. Compress it.

Each recurring exception should be classified before it is digitized. Some variation should be eliminated. Some should be standardized. Some needs assistance. Some needs explicit escalation. Some must retain human judgment. The point is not maximum automation; it is lower recurrence with clearer authority.

Decision architecture

What becomes easier to decide?

A credible operating model changes attention and authority. It identifies which friction enters the portfolio, which intervention fits, who owns the workflow, what proof is sufficient, and what should stop.

Where to intervene

Distinguish root recurrence from downstream symptom. Start where data, policy, handoff, or authority creates repeated avoidable work.

Exception recurrence

What to standardize

Standardize decisions and data contracts that protect customers and controls; retain legitimate product, market, and relationship variation.

First-pass quality

What to automate

Automate stable rules and evidence movement. Assist ambiguous work. Escalate consequential exceptions. Keep human authority explicit.

Touchless vs assisted mix

What to fund or stop

Fund interventions with visible workflow ownership, bounded risk, adoption capacity, and measurable value. Stop solutions that move burden rather than reduce it.

Value realization

When to reuse

Reuse only when the decision logic, data boundary, operating conditions, and ownership model transfer - not merely because the technology does.

Executive interview questions

Questions that reveal the real mandate.

These questions are designed to test the operating hypothesis, expose decision boundaries, and understand where the Digital Factory must create trust before it creates scale.

  1. Which OTC exceptions create the greatest customer or patient-care consequence today, and which create the greatest internal burden?

  2. Where does J&J need global consistency, and where must local market or product judgment remain intentionally different?

  3. How are Digital Factory products currently owned after launch - by the factory, the value stream, a platform team, or a shared model?

  4. What evidence causes the organization to scale, redesign, partner, or stop a digital intervention?

  5. Where is the strongest source of transformation fatigue or skepticism, and what has earned trust in the past?

  6. How does the planned Orthopaedics separation change the near-term OTC landscape, dependencies, or continuity burden for this role?
