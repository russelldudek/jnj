This record supports the independent candidate campaign for **Director, MedTech Digital Factory - OTC**. Company identity establishes who the work is for; the original **Order Trace** system establishes Russell Dudek's point of view. The campaign does not imply sponsorship, authorship, approval, employment, or endorsement by Johnson & Johnson.

## Official identity decision

Johnson & Johnson's current public identity uses a modern red sans-serif corporate wordmark and the MedTech business name. The available connected research tools exposed the official pages but did not expose a stable, crisp standalone logo file that could be downloaded and locally committed without tracing, redrawing, extracting a restricted webfont, or relying on a third-party logo repository.

Because the campaign must not redraw or approximate an official mark, it uses a **clearly typeset nominative company identity** instead of a counterfeit or third-party logo asset:

> Johnson & Johnson | MedTech  
> Candidate vision for Director, MedTech Digital Factory - OTC  
> by Russell Dudek

The typeset name is not represented as the official logo. It appears above the fold and in restrained document furniture, always paired with an independent-candidate qualifier. The candidate-created Order Trace mark remains a secondary role-metaphor asset and never substitutes for employer identity.

## Typography decision

The official web pages present a modern, readable sans-serif identity. The connected sources did not expose a licensed, reusable official font package or a sufficiently reliable official font declaration. No proprietary webfont binary was copied.

The campaign therefore uses the documented licensed/system substitute stack:

`Helvetica Neue`, `Aptos`, `Liberation Sans`, `Arial`, `sans-serif`

This choice mirrors the contemporary humanist/grotesque sans character through weight, tracking, compact hierarchy, and high legibility. Arial is a fallback only, not an unexplained design default. Display and body typography intentionally share the same sans family to avoid reverting to the prior editorial serif treatment that did not match the current corporate identity.

## Photography, illustration, product, and interface cues

No official photography is embedded because the available sources did not provide a clearly reusable downloadable media asset that materially improved the role argument. The campaign instead creates original, role-specific diagrams using documented cues from the public identity:

- disciplined red/white/charcoal recognition layer;
- clean clinical precision and generous white space;
- thin rules, clear hierarchy, and accessible information density;
- restrained motion with smooth path behavior and deliberate state changes;
- MedTech context expressed through accountable flow, evidence, quality, and customer commitments rather than generic healthcare stock imagery.

## Motion character

The official digital presence feels calm, precise, contemporary, and human rather than mechanical or theatrical. The campaign translates that influence into slow Order Trace packet movement, a single decision marker, and direct scenario transitions. This motion is candidate-original and role-derived, not copied from a company animation.
